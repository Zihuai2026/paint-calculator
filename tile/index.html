How to Work Out How Many Tiles You Need

Tiles are sold by the piece or by the box, never by the square foot — so the job is to convert your floor area into a piece count, then add a waste allowance for cuts around walls, doorways and any pattern you are laying.

1

Measure the floor

Length × width in feet gives the area. Split L-shaped rooms into two rectangles and add them.

2

Convert tile size

Multiply the tile's two sides in inches, then divide by 144 to get square feet per tile.

3

Add waste

10% for a straight layout, 15–20% for diagonal, herringbone or rooms with many corners.

4

Round up twice

Round up to whole tiles, then round that up to whole boxes — you cannot buy part of a box.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tiles do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 × 12 ft room is 144 sq ft. With 10% waste that is 158.4 sq ft. Using 12 × 12 inch tiles (1 sq ft each) you need 159 tiles; with 12 × 24 inch tiles (2 sq ft each) you need 80 tiles.

How much extra tile should I buy?

10% is standard for a simple rectangular room. Use 15% for diagonal layouts, herringbone patterns or rooms with many corners and doorways. Keeping a few spare tiles is also wise for future repairs.

How many square feet does a box of tile cover?

It varies by tile size and brand. A box usually lists both the piece count and the coverage. Common examples: 12 × 12 inch tiles about 10 sq ft per box, 12 × 24 inch tiles about 16 sq ft per box. Always check the carton.
