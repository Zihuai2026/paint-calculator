How to Work Out How Many Boxes of Flooring You Need

Flooring is sold by the box, but rooms are not box-shaped. The gap between "room area" and "boxes to buy" is the waste factor — the extra material consumed by end cuts, doorways and damaged planks.

1

Measure the room

Length × width in feet gives the floor area. Split L-shaped rooms into two rectangles and add them.

2

Add waste

10% for a standard room, 15–20% for many corners or a diagonal layout.

3

Check the box coverage

Laminate boxes usually cover 20–25 sq ft; vinyl plank boxes 18–30 sq ft. It's printed on the carton.

4

Round up

Divide the area with waste by the coverage, then always round up — you cannot buy half a box.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many boxes of flooring do I need?

Floor area = length × width. Add 10% for cuts and waste, then divide by the square footage each box covers and round up. A 12 × 12 ft room (144 sq ft) with 10% waste and 24.5 sq ft per box needs 7 boxes.

How much waste should I add?

10% is standard for a simple rectangular room. Use 15% for rooms with many corners, diagonal layouts or lots of doorways, and 5% for tile with a simple straight layout.

Do I need underlayment?

Laminate and many vinyl planks need an underlayment for sound and moisture protection, usually sold in rolls covering 100 sq ft. Buy one roll per 100 sq ft of floor area, rounded up.
