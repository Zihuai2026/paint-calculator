How to Work Out How Many Sheets of Drywall You Need

Drywall is sold in whole sheets, so the job is to turn your wall and ceiling area into an area with a waste allowance, then divide by the square footage of one sheet and round up. Guessing "about twenty sheets" is how weekend projects end up making two trips to the lumber yard.

1

Measure the room

Length, width and ceiling height. Walls are the perimeter times the height: 2 × (L + W) × H.

2

Add or skip the ceiling

A ceiling adds L × W. Skip it if you are only re-drywalling walls.

3

Subtract openings

A standard door is about 21 sq ft, a window about 15 sq ft. Two of each is roughly 42 sq ft.

4

Add waste, then round up

10% covers normal cuts. 15–20% for rooms with many openings or odd shapes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 x 12 ft room with an 8 ft ceiling has 384 sq ft of wall and 144 sq ft of ceiling, so 528 sq ft total. Subtract about 42 sq ft for two doors and two windows to get 486 sq ft. Add 10% waste and divide by 32 sq ft per 4 x 8 sheet — you need 17 sheets.

How much joint compound do I need?

Plan on about one gallon of ready-mix joint compound per 100 sq ft of drywall for taping plus three coats. A 486 sq ft room needs roughly 5 gallons, which is two 4.5-gallon buckets. Level 5 finishes and heavy texture need more.

Should I use 1/2 inch or 5/8 inch drywall?

1/2 inch is standard for walls and ceilings on 16 inch stud spacing. Use 5/8 inch for ceilings on 24 inch spacing, for fire-rated walls between a garage and the house, and where you want less sag and better sound control. 5/8 inch weighs more and costs more per sheet.
