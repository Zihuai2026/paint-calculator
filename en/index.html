How to Calculate How Much Paint You Need

Painting a room is mostly math, not guesswork. Buying too little means a second trip to the store with a half-painted wall and a slightly different color batch; buying too much wastes $30–$60 a gallon. The four steps below are exactly what this calculator runs for you in real time.

1

Measure the room

Measure the length, width and ceiling height in feet. A tape measure laid along the baseboard gives you length and width.

2

Add up all four walls

Wall area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height. This is the perimeter of the room multiplied by the ceiling height.

3

Subtract doors and windows

A standard door is about 20 sq ft and a typical window 12–15 sq ft. You don't paint over glass, so subtract them.

4

Divide by coverage

One gallon covers roughly 400 sq ft per coat. Divide the wall area by 400, multiply by the number of coats, and round up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many gallons of paint do I need for a room?

Wall area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height − doors and windows area. One gallon covers about 400 sq ft per coat. With the recommended 2 coats: gallons = wall area × 2 ÷ 400, rounded up. A typical 12 × 12 ft bedroom needs about 3 gallons.

Why is a second coat recommended?

The first coat primes and covers, the second ensures even, saturated color. Dark colors or big color changes may even need 3 coats — especially when going from a dark wall to a light one.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Yes. A standard door is about 20 sq ft and a typical window 12–15 sq ft. Enter the total area and the calculator subtracts it automatically.

Do I need primer?

Primer is not included in this calculation. Bare drywall, patched walls, dark-to-light color changes and stained surfaces all need a primer coat first — add roughly 1 gallon per 400 sq ft of wall area.

How much extra paint should I buy?

10% extra is a common buffer for touch-ups and roller absorption, and it keeps you inside a single color batch. Textured or porous walls can soak up 15–20% more than the calculator estimate.
